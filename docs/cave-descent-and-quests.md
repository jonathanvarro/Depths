# One cave, many depths — campaign and activity proposal

2026-10-19 · Design proposal [#125](https://github.com/jonathanvarro/Depths/issues/125) · Story [#122](https://github.com/jonathanvarro/Depths/issues/122) · Quest packages [#123](https://github.com/jonathanvarro/Depths/issues/123) · Fallen companions [#107](https://github.com/jonathanvarro/Depths/issues/107).

**Owner direction:** concentrate the game around the cave, its surroundings and the camp; descend through increasingly intricate floors toward a relic/deepest objective. Camp NPCs give visible quests; hidden activities use authored areas inserted into generated floors. The queen is a side quest, not the campaign ending. A rival team begins in competition; its motives may become understandable later. The relic's identity, commander's true nature and cause of the ambush remain open.

**Proposal below:** twelve floors in six layers, working place/quest names, 48 activity concepts, materials and story connections. These are options for review, not approved lore, a required production list or implemented gameplay. A temple and portal are a possible finale presentation; neither defines the relic yet. No new game rules, floors, mining, quests or story scenes are added by this document.

## The recommended shape

Make the cave feel like **one place with a buried history**, rather than unrelated environments stacked below one another. People occupy its entrances; goblins control the passages and waterworks; water feeds fungal growth through old conduits; those conduits lead into a buried city; the city's deepest structures surround a temple and something still active.

The surface camp remains the familiar home. Each return brings practical rewards and another interpretation of what the party found. Familiar NPCs become invested in particular discoveries. The descent changes the player's understanding of the commander, rivals and original ambush without prematurely proving who is guilty.

| Floor | Working name | Identity / signature landmark | Main route toward the next depth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Old Workings | Pick scars, broken rails, abandoned supply station | Trace an old survey route to the deeper fissure |
| 2 | The Webbed Chasm | Natural galleries, webs, a broken hoist over a rift | Restore a crossing or use the longer inhabited ledge |
| 3 | The Goblin Marches | Watch posts, prisoners, occupied natural caves | Learn a route through the fortress approaches |
| 4 | The Queen's Hold | A fortress built into much older stone | Reach the lower water gate; the queen's hall is optional |
| 5 | The Drowned Galleries | Flooded tunnels, still pools, abandoned drains | Make a dry passage through the old drainage network |
| 6 | The Black Reservoir | A large cistern, sluices, something moving below | Divert the flow to expose the descent |
| 7 | The Mosslit Hollows | Moss-covered stone, roots, luminous growth | Follow maintenance channels through the living growth |
| 8 | The Garden of Echoes | Fungal terraces occupying an ancient conservatory | Open a route through the garden to the city boundary |
| 9 | The Buried Threshold | A city gate, streets carved beneath rock, unknown writing | Understand one public gate mechanism and enter the inner ward |
| 10 | The Silent Ward | Homes, archives, a dead foundry, signs of evacuation | Find a route and local instructions to the temple precinct |
| 11 | The Temple of Seals | Ritual chambers, pilgrim passages, damaged wards | Complete the temple's own sequence to reach its heart |
| 12 | The Last Threshold | The relic chamber and a possible portal | Reach the objective and resolve what should happen to it |

Six coordinated environment families are enough for this outline. The two floors in each family should differ through landmarks, route structure, inhabitants and activities, rather than requiring completely separate art libraries. Bleed themes across boundaries: carved stone on floor 2, a blocked water channel on floor 3, roots on floor 6, fungal traces on city walls.

The numbers are a planning scaffold. If twelve floors stretches the content too thin, combine each pair into one larger chapter before adding filler. First validate floors 1–4 and one convincing glimpse below the fortress; the rest is the direction for expansion.

## What a floor contains

Aim for **one recognizable landmark and one clear route onward**, supported by a small selection of optional activities:

- A primary traversal/story area, offering at least one reliable way onward.
- One active camp-given quest package, chosen from eligible activities.
- Zero or one hidden package, discovered through consistent environmental clues.
- One or two optional resource opportunities, not a mining node in every room.
- Ordinary encounters, supplies and relief spaces that connect these places.
- Where appropriate, a route improvement the party can secure for later returns.

The four activities listed for each floor are a **library**, not four compulsory tasks in every generated layout. Start with one visible and one hidden activity, sharing a resource opportunity where useful. Side quests can be declined. The primary route must not depend on finding a hidden quest, bringing one class, accepting a faction's morality, or winning an unrepeatable coin flip.

Difficulty should increase through route choices, interacting threats and consequences as well as enemy strength. Introduce water crossings before a puzzle with changing water levels; readable spores before a choice about harvesting their source; simple inscriptions before the temple's multi-step sequence. Avoid a global swimming/oxygen simulation or a separate minigame for every biome.

## Floors 1–2: familiar danger, personal reasons to return

### Floor 1 — The Old Workings

**Feel:** recently abandoned human activity at the edge of something much older. Rats, lone spiders and unstable workings make the player's equipment and light matter. A half-finished survey station is the landmark.

**Story contribution:** survivors and supplies did not simply become lost. Small details suggest the original party's route was known to someone. They are clues, not proof of the ambusher's identity.

| Activity | Origin | Playable package | Reward / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Those Left Behind** | Visible: commander or camp conversation | Revisit the opening party's remains, identify them, secure the chamber and choose what to bring back as evidence. A camp recovery crew can handle burial after the area is secured. | A personal camp scene, recovered keepsakes and the first ambush evidence. Resurrection and the fate of any individual are not decided here. |
| **A Box of Clean Linen** | Visible: nurse | Find a lost medical crate. Open it for immediate field supplies or deliver it intact; both choices acknowledge what the player did. | Treatment-stock opportunity or supplies used to keep this expedition alive. No arbitrary permanent failure for opening the box. |
| **Three Knocks** | Hidden: tapping behind rubble | A short collapsed branch contains a trapped miner. Brace a passage and extract them, or mark the location and request a recovery team. | A grateful camp contact, a route hint and a vein location. No unnoticed real-time death countdown. |
| **Iron for the Apprentice** | Visible: smith, once he arrives | Take a small sample from a marked seam, with an optional noisy attempt for an extra bundle. | Unlock a modest smith project; the first practical reason to return with material rather than sell everything. |

**Route:** survey markings point to a deeper fissure. **Later revisit:** a secured winch station can reduce the first part of the commute.

The live tutorial is a separate floor-0 sequence. Reconnecting its bodies room as an annex off an early floor is a proposed story/world-layout change, not permission to teleport the bodies or duplicate tutorial loot. Preserve the original identities and evidence with stable quest state. It must work for Mara, Corin and Alwen, since only the chosen main hero exists in that campaign.

### Floor 2 — The Webbed Chasm

**Feel:** bigger natural spaces, web curtains, ledges, a failed mining lift and distant goblin signals. The broken hoist spans a readable landmark rift, while alternate passages keep navigation fair.

**Story contribution:** the organized traffic below makes the ambush more plausible, but human tracks and goblin markings do not yet establish who gave the order.

| Activity | Origin | Playable package | Reward / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Patrol That Never Returned** | Visible: commander | Follow two survey markers into an isolated patrol camp. Recover their route notes and decide whether to pursue the missing scout into a dangerous branch. | Fortress approach information; a survivor or camp conversation if the branch is completed. |
| **The Spider-Keeper** | Hidden: deliberately arranged food scraps | An isolated scavenger lives beside a webbed nesting area. Talk, trade food, take another route or fight; their explanation is useful but self-interested. | A way past one nest and a hint about deeper water; no universal spider immunity. |
| **A Thread of Silver** | Hidden: a metallic glint distinct from silk | Follow a wire through a trapped alcove. Patient inspection exposes its mechanism and a hidden cache. | A valuable or utility item, plus an example of a clue that materially changes danger. |
| **The Broken Hoist** | Visible: apprentice / survey notes | Recover a missing brake and fit it at the hoist, or clear the longer ledge path. | A persistent route improvement. Required descent has a tool-free alternative. |

**Route:** descend by repaired hoist or the ledge. **Transition:** goblin stakes and drums appear before the next floor's arrival.

## Floors 3–4: an occupied fortress, not the bottom of the world

### Floor 3 — The Goblin Marches

**Feel:** caves adapted into guard posts, cages, store rooms and crude barricades. Patrol patterns and the defenders' relationships matter more than a simple HP increase.

**Story contribution:** the goblins are organized, but they are also guarding against something below. Their control of passages creates opportunities to bargain or infiltrate without making either universally safe.

| Activity | Origin | Playable package | Reward / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Quiet Way In** | Visible: commander or a rescued scout | Examine a supply entrance, abandoned drain and guarded gate; choose an approach after learning each cost. | A reliable fortress route and a camp briefing. Avoiding the gate is legitimate progress. |
| **Names on a Slate** | Visible: a prisoner's relative or camp recruit | Find a holding pen, identify the captives and secure an exit. Freeing one frightened captive may make the immediate route harder. | A rescued camp character or useful testimony; a bounded escort across the package, not an escort through every floor. |
| **The Goblin Who Trades** | Hidden: a deliberate cairn and offerings | A goblin broker trades practical supplies and information. Honour a small exchange, steal or leave. | A conditional local contact and a hint about the queen's lower gate. No mandatory faction/reputation meter. |
| **Tools of Occupation** | Visible: smith | Recover a crate of seized tools from a guarded store or intercept its local transport. | A specific smith capability or iron stock; choose useful recovery over unrelated kill counts. |

**Route:** multiple approaches lead to the next floor. **Optional relief:** a secured side chamber can become a limited rest/relay point, not a second town.

### Floor 4 — The Queen's Hold

**Feel:** the known goblin fortress, with older stonework beneath timber and iron. The queen's hall remains a significant, warned encounter and can reuse existing content.

**Story contribution:** the queen controls a strategic junction, not the campaign's final truth. Records, plumbing and rival activity establish that the desired object is farther down.

| Activity | Origin | Playable package | Reward / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Queen's Claim** | Visible: commander / bounty | Enter the queen's guarded hall and defeat her. Keep the existing warning and commitment to that fight. | A major payout, a local power change and a safer optional route. Does not end the campaign. |
| **A Gate Without a Battle** | Visible: goblin broker, scout or rival clue | Find the service route and operate a separate lower-gate mechanism through a maintenance package. | The main descent remains possible without killing the queen. Do not make an optional boss mandatory through a disguised key requirement. |
| **The Queen's Ledger** | Hidden: a disturbed record chest | Recover records about payments, stolen surveys and lower passages. There may be contradictions between witnesses and the ledger. | New questions for the commander and rivals; the documents do not automatically prove the commander guilty. |
| **The Forge Below** | Visible: apprentice | Recover a heat-treatment diagram and usable bellows parts from an outlying forge. | A modest, named equipment option; the apprentice does not instantly become a master smith. |

**Route:** the lower water gate, reached through the service route or an optional conquered fortress route. **Milestone:** the camera view reveals a flooded gallery and ancient stone channel. The queen is now one story arc within the descent.

## Floors 5–6: water is a route problem and a clue

### Floor 5 — The Drowned Galleries

**Feel:** ankle-deep passages, black pools, dry ledges and drowned stores. Use visible pools and stateful paths; this need not become an underwater swimming game.

**Story contribution:** the fortress's water is part of an older engineered system. Goblins inherited controls they only partly understand.

| Activity | Origin | Playable package | Reward / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Missing Water-Carriers** | Visible: nurse | Locate a stranded supply party on a dry shelf. Reopen their path or arrange extraction after securing the package. | A camp relationship, medicine supplies and an account of something in the water. |
| **Coins in the Water** | Hidden: repeated flashes beneath a pool | Investigate a submerged strongbox. Lower the local water, use a prepared retrieval tool or leave it. A careless grab has a warned cost. | Treasure or a tool; no mandatory breath-stat system. |
| **A Dry Flame** | Visible: merchant / smith | Bring a sealing-resin sample and test a protected light housing at a harmless splash point. | Access to a waterproofing/utility equipment option. The basic lantern remains useful elsewhere. |
| **The Bell Under the Pool** | Hidden: a bell sounding without an obvious rope | Follow an accessible ledge to its mechanism and expose the top of an old relief. | A clue linking the channels to the city and a small cache or route marker. |

**Route:** choose a longer dry walkway or open a short drainage bypass. Essential paths do not require a rare randomly dropped utility item.

### Floor 6 — The Black Reservoir

**Feel:** a large central cistern, valves and walkways make the floor easy to recognize. Choosing which channel to open changes a small, authored part of the floor.

**Story contribution:** the waterworks served a population far larger than the goblins. Their symbols repeat in the growth-covered passages below.

| Activity | Origin | Playable package | Reward / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Open the Sluice** | Visible: expedition briefing / valve markings | Trace two nearby controls and divert water from the descent. Different settings open a safe route or a risky cache branch. | Access downward and a persistent local route solution; no basin-wide fluid simulation. |
| **Something in the Cistern** | Visible: nurse | Compare samples at two outlets, identify the contaminated source and seal that local feeder. A creature or nest may defend it. | A useful treatment ingredient and relief for rescued workers; no automatic global camp sickness system. |
| **Words Through the Grate** | Hidden: a voice behind a sluice | Reach an injured member of the rival team. Help, bargain for information, or report their location. | First personal contact with the rivals, an owed favour or a guarded account of their purpose. |
| **The Drowned Workshop** | Visible: smith | Retrieve a pump drawing and parts from a workshop accessible through the chosen water route. | A camp-supported supply relay/route project, with a clear unlock rather than a pile of anonymous scrap. |

**Route:** the main sluice reveals a descent into root- and moss-lined conduits. **Relief:** repair a dry maintenance platform as a limited recovery stop.

## Floors 7–8: life has taken over something artificial

### Floor 7 — The Mosslit Hollows

**Feel:** patches of soft light, soaked stone, hanging roots and unusually warm hollows. Relief and danger are both legible; not every inviting space should be a trick.

**Story contribution:** the growth follows precisely cut channels. This is a living ecosystem inside old infrastructure, not just a disconnected mushroom biome.

| Activity | Origin | Playable package | Reward / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A Cure That Takes Root** | Visible: nurse | Find a named medicinal growth from a drawn description. Compare a safe sample with an irritant growing nearby before cutting. | A treatment recipe or improved supply availability; effects still follow outside-combat recovery rules. |
| **The Warm Alcove** | Hidden: a steady warm draught | Find a sheltered hollow, clear a small obstruction and verify the clean air outlet. | A real limited rest opportunity and a calmer companion scene. The clue should earn dependable relief. |
| **The Resin Tappers** | Visible: smith / old workers' marks | Collect sealing resin from a colony or root seep, choosing a slow careful harvest or a noisy larger cut. | Waterproofing/project material; no requirement to harvest every plant. |
| **Footprints Without Feet** | Hidden: disturbed luminous moss | Follow impressions between three nearby surfaces. Light reveals an old repeating pattern beneath the growth. | A city symbol and a clue about earlier visitors; the exact supernatural explanation remains open. |

**Route:** maintenance channels through the growth. **Threat escalation:** enemy combinations make control and protection important, without introducing a separate disease system.

### Floor 8 — The Garden of Echoes

**Feel:** giant fungal beds fill stepped stone basins, with fragments of railings and carved plant containers. It gradually becomes clear that this was once a tended space.

**Story contribution:** the ancient population maintained this place, and someone else has recently been trying to read its marks. The rivals know something the commander has not shared.

| Activity | Origin | Playable package | Reward / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Garden That Remembers** | Visible: an antiquarian visiting camp | Compare three carved bed markers with impressions found on floor 7. The package supplies the necessary local clues even if the earlier hidden quest was missed. | An interpretation of the city's boundary and a useful route hint. |
| **The Gardener** | Hidden: tools arranged beside a cleared bed | Discover a surviving keeper, strange custodian or other inhabitant. Help restore one feeder, bargain, leave or fight. Its actual nature is a story choice. | Safe local passage or a noncombat reward; no required adoption of a whole new playable faction. |
| **The Rival's Wounded** | Visible after contact: rival representative | Escort an injured rival across a short contested garden branch, or retrieve the supplies they need. | A personal favour and a more credible account of the rivalry, with a reaction at camp. |
| **No More Spores** | Visible: merchant / expedition workers | Open a blocked vent affecting one route. Decide whether to preserve a valuable growth bed or clear the route more aggressively. | A shorter/cleaner local passage or extra resin; a comprehensible trade-off, not an invisible permanent debuff. |

**Route:** a garden service arch exposes the city gate. **Milestone:** a first clear view of streets and buildings under the rock, rather than an abrupt loading-screen change.

## Floors 9–10: an unknown city, not a solved history lesson

### Floor 9 — The Buried Threshold

**Feel:** a gate too large for the present inhabitants, streets disappearing beneath debris, unfamiliar proportions and repeated symbols. The city should have recognizable homes and public places without immediately explaining who built it.

**Story contribution:** what the camp calls a relic belonged to a wider social or protective system. Evidence supports multiple readings; its final function stays open.

| Activity | Origin | Playable package | Reward / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Name of This Place** | Visible: antiquarian | Make rubbings from a boundary stone, civic marker and water plaque. Compare their repeated elements. | A partial name or concept, not instant translation of the entire language; better questions at camp. |
| **A Door for the Living** | Hidden: clean marks on an otherwise dusty doorway | Solve a local entry test using its own nearby clues. It may distinguish a living visitor from a dead mechanism, rather than demand a particular hero. | A sanctuary/cache and a demonstration of the city's rules. |
| **The House with One Lamp** | Hidden: a light in an abandoned dwelling | Enter a small domestic scene preserved or disturbed in an unusual way. Recover a personal object or choose to leave it respectfully. | A character moment, a valuable or evidence of a recent occupant. No predetermined ghost lore is required. |
| **Metal That Will Not Rust** | Visible: smith | Bring a fragment from a collapsed public fitting, rather than destroying an active ward or unique artefact. | Unlock an elder-alloy equipment experiment, with the associated scarcity and smith skill still visible. |

**Route:** operate a public gate by observing repeated local patterns. An optional language quest helps interpret it but is not the only way through.

### Floor 10 — The Silent Ward

**Feel:** workshops, archives, dwelling clusters and an abandoned foundry. Layouts become more interlinked, with named landmarks and loops instead of a large field of similar corridors.

**Story contribution:** evacuation orders, repairs and conflicting records explain what the city was trying to manage. They also complicate the commander's claim that the object can simply be carried away.

| Activity | Origin | Playable package | Reward / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Broken Archive** | Visible: antiquarian | Reassemble a small set of records from adjacent damaged chambers, deciding which fragile record to save first. | A reliable piece of history and a temple clue; enough local information remains for the main route if this side quest is missed. |
| **An Order Never Delivered** | Visible: commander's attendant | Find a missing dispatch carried by a later expedition. Compare it with orders already heard at camp. | Dialogue with the commander or attendant. Contradictions can imply manipulation without deciding whether the author, messenger or recipient is guilty. |
| **The Last Watch** | Hidden: a warning repeated through empty rooms | Reach a custodian still observing an old instruction. Interpret, satisfy or confront it using clues inside its package. | A shortcut or protective utility item. A conversation can be as consequential as a fight. |
| **The Foundry's Memory** | Visible: apprentice | Recover one workable pattern and a small alloy sample from a dead foundry. Restart only a local test mechanism if worthwhile. | A named quality equipment option or fitting service, not an infinite metal/gold factory. |

**Route:** an old processional/service passage leads to the temple precinct. **Relief:** a surveyed station can become the last camp-supported supply relay, subject to securing its approach.

## Floors 11–12: obligations, interpretation and a finale

### Floor 11 — The Temple of Seals

**Feel:** damaged inscriptions, ritual routes, older machinery and pilgrims' side passages. It should feel connected to the city's streets and water/garden systems, not like an unrelated endgame castle.

**Story contribution:** the player has enough context to question what the temple is protecting or containing. The commander and rivals offer interpretations that can be examined against evidence.

| Activity | Origin | Playable package | Reward / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Three Observances** | Main route: instructions in the temple | Complete three nearby actions tied to the temple's own rules, such as restoring a water channel, setting a named marker and opening a witness alcove. These are examples, not final theology. | Access to the deepest chamber. All necessary clues/components exist locally; optional quests supply understanding or alternatives. |
| **The Sword at the Door** | Hidden: a familiar royal insignia | Find someone close to the commander attempting to reach or stop him. Help them recover their resolve, learn their doubts or leave them to their own plan. | An ally/contact or an alternate account. Their relationship and the commander's fate remain open; do not copy a reference game's plot. |
| **Wardglass** | Visible: smith / antiquarian | Collect a safely detached fragment and compare it with one intact ward. Taking more would require a clearly warned destructive choice. | A late protective/utility project, or knowledge gained by preserving the intact ward. Main progression never requires damaging it. |
| **The Rival's Account** | Visible: rival representative | Meet with the team and test its claim against an inscription, object or earlier testimony. Choose what evidence to share. | A possible agreement, a guarded truce or continued rivalry. Understanding their motive does not automatically prove them wholly right. |

**Route:** the temple sequence opens the last descent. **Preparation:** the game clearly signals the return opportunity and any upcoming commitment; no surprise permanent lock-in.

### Floor 12 — The Last Threshold

**Feel:** a compact, authored climax surrounded by a few exploration spaces. A relic chamber and possible portal provide a focal point; the emotional and strategic stakes matter more than the largest maze.

**Story contribution:** the accumulated evidence turns the deepest objective into a choice with consequences. The relic's actual identity and the portal's nature must be decided before final writing/art; both remain open in this proposal.

| Activity | Origin | Playable package | Reward / consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **What Lies Beyond** | Main objective | Reach the chamber, understand enough of the local mechanism and resolve its central encounter. Taking, sealing, stabilizing or surrendering the object are possible ending structures to choose from, not four promised branches. | Campaign resolution and a return/epilogue structure. Reaching the bottom alone should not leave the player's purpose unclear. |
| **The Unclaimed Dead** | Hidden: matching marks or a recovered keepsake | Discover evidence that reframes the first party's loss. A modest memorial or return of a token can close their story. | Personal closure and a companion reaction, rather than a powerful item that makes earlier grief a loot puzzle. |
| **The Commander's Last Order** | Visible: commander or a messenger | Face a final demand concerning the object. Comply, negotiate or oppose it according to the chosen story structure and evidence. | A relationship/ending consequence. A commander boss fight is only an option if his eventual role warrants it. |
| **A Light for the Way Back** | Visible after reaching the chamber | Secure a return anchor, stabilize the retreat corridor or help a stranded expedition member. Show the cost before commitment. | A safer exit and a way to bring more people home. Optional rescue must not make the only viable completion depend on a missed side quest. |

The finale should acknowledge actual choices without requiring dozens of fully separate endings. A few state-aware epilogue lines at camp can show who survived, what was learned and who controls the objective.

## Three continuing quest threads

**The fallen party and the ambush:** recover/secure remains (1) → missing patrol evidence (2) → prisoner testimony (3) → ledger (4) → a city dispatch (10) → a personal answer or honest remaining uncertainty (12). At least one reliable clue must sit on the main route, so a player who misses hidden areas still understands the central conflict. Evidence must distinguish fact, interpretation and contradiction; do not let one random note settle a major betrayal.

**The rivals:** distant signs (2–4) → a wounded person who needs help (6) → a reciprocal task (8) → competing evidence (9–10) → a serious conversation (11) → the final stance (12). Dark elves are the owner's current rival concept, not a reason to make every encounter hostile. They can compete, trade, obstruct or sometimes cooperate. Do not set an invisible real-time race that loses the relic while the player reads or rests.

**The buried civilization:** reused stones (2–4) → water engineering (5–6) → tended biological growth (7–8) → lived-in streets and incomplete records (9–10) → instructions and obligations (11–12). Reveal what the civilization did before deciding its full identity. Individual domestic details should carry some story alongside large inscriptions.

## Camp NPCs and why quests matter

Existing camp services can carry most activity rewards: the nurse cares about people and medical supplies, the apprentice about tools and workable patterns, the merchant about recoverable goods and safe routes, and recruits about comrades or fears. The commander, an attendant, an antiquarian and a rival representative are possible additional story roles; not all need a permanent new camp destination. Use visiting conversations in existing activity views before adding buildings.

Reward variety matters:

- **Progress:** a route, a clue, access or a secured local operation.
- **Practical gain:** supplies, gold, one useful gear option, a recruit/contact or a material project.
- **Personal gain:** an answer, a remembered rescue, a recovered keepsake or an honest disagreement.

Not every quest needs all three. A hidden quest can be short and worthwhile without becoming a full quest-log chain. A nurse's supply request should help the camp in a visible, bounded way rather than secretly raising all prices or healing everyone's injuries instantly.

Use the current story-driven merchant/smith arrival rules. The smith cannot give his first job before he arrives, and the merchant need not open trading early to give a non-trading line. Quest giver availability should change the conversation, not erase already accepted work. The chosen hero's name/pronouns/reactions must match their campaign; unchosen main heroes do not become waiting quest givers.

## Gathering and camp projects: a small first system

Recommend **project unlocks**, with a few material categories and named recipes, before a full crafting tree. The player returns a sample/bundle plus any required drawing; the apprentice or nurse learns one bounded option. Gold can still pay for making/buying the finished item, so materials and the existing economy have different jobs.

| Material family | Suggested depths | Example project | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Iron ore / usable iron stock | 1–4 | Better basic weapon option or simple tool project | A small one-time delivery; do not silently turn existing sale-only copper ore into a mandatory new currency |
| Sealing resin | 5–8 | Waterproofed kit or protective equipment fitting | Utility/sidegrade; not required to navigate the main route |
| Elder alloy | 9–10 | A rare equipment pattern or repair/fitting option | Salvage from safe fragments; the apprentice still has limited capabilities |
| Wardglass | 11–12 | A late protective/utility option | A detached fragment is enough; no forced destruction of the finale's wards |

Medicinal moss/fungi can initially be named quest samples used by the nurse, not a fifth stackable crafting economy. Precious stones remain treasure unless a specific recipe gives them another use.

A mining node should offer a small decision: **take the exposed sample quickly**, **spend more time for a larger yield with a warned local risk**, or **leave it**. The cost can be time, light, noise, an encounter or a clearly signalled unstable face. Avoid a separate reflex mining minigame and hidden random cave-ins. Any hero can perform the basic action; an eventual role/tool bonus can make it safer or faster without blocking progress.

At camp, show the project, requirement, delivered progress and resulting option. Depositing material makes it safe under the existing stash principle; completed unlocks survive wipes and daily regeneration. Carried optional material can follow ordinary carried-item loss. Essential story access/evidence must have an explicit recoverable fallback, not disappear forever with a dead recruit.

Start with **one iron project**, such as a small sample delivery plus usable tools. Choose and test its exact quantities, gold cost and weapon effect against the current economy before adding all four families. A steady loop of harvesting low-risk seams must not be a better progression path than discovering deeper content. Repeatable material yields and sale values need a deliberate economy policy; no infinite full-value quest payout for the same node.

## Authored activity packages inside generated floors

The current generator makes 31 × 31 floors with up to nine mostly small rooms; current room events are mostly one-cell choices. A multi-room activity is a new generator/content layer, not merely a longer `RoomEvent` string.

An activity package should contain:

- Stable ID, allowed layer/floors, eligibility conditions and completion states.
- A modest authored shape, for example 2–4 rooms, with one or two connection sockets; allowed rotations that preserve puzzle meaning and readability.
- A landmark/clue, interactables, local encounter/choices and an explicit resolution/reward.
- Reserved entry/exit paths, local safe/blocked areas, and a fallback if it cannot be placed.
- Any dependency on another package or guaranteed clue, declared before layout generation.
- Persistent NPC/evidence/node state, plus a rule for showing a resolved area on revisits.

Place required traversal/landmark packages first, then eligible quest packages, then build connecting rooms/corridors and ordinary contents around them. Keep optional packages out of the only route to stairs. A mining alcove can be one room; a sluice puzzle may need three. Budget by footprint and connections, not only by counting quests, and do not assume two large prefabs will fit the existing floor size.

Where a small map cannot fit optional content, retry placement or defer that optional package to a supported floor. **Never silently omit an accepted required quest target.** Provide a validated fallback layout for the essential route. Packages should have enough alternative approaches to remain interesting in new surrounding layouts, while their internal clues and solutions stay coherent.

### Daily regeneration without forgetting the story

Preserve the decided daily cave regeneration unless the owner later changes it. Store narrative state by **quest/package ID**, separate from the current floor seed and cell coordinates. New layouts can change the approach while preserving the rescued miner, delivered sample, broken gate, opened sluice and spent one-time reward.

Use a simple lifecycle: unavailable → eligible → discovered/offered → active → resolved, with branch outcome flags where needed. Quest log entries show known location/clue, next action and whom to report to. Hidden content is absent from the log until actually discovered; no per-floor question-mark checklist is the recommendation, not a settled interface rule.

If the floor already exists when a camp quest is accepted, do not conjure a room into explored walls. Prefer placing currently eligible world packages before the player accepts their quest, with the NPC directing attention to something already there. Reserve dormant sockets for later story activation. A genuinely new package that cannot activate safely must have an honest availability rule at the next suitable regeneration; do not promise an immediately present target that the generator has not placed.

Completed optional areas may remain as cleared scenery, with reward/occupants updated, or be omitted in future layouts if nothing depends on them. Completed traversal solutions and route unlocks must still offer the promised passage. One-time XP/gold/material awards use a persistent claim record, including across a save during resolution. Repeated hostile occupants, if desired, do not resurrect rescued NPCs or reset their story rewards.

Essential clues must have more than one source: a main-route observation plus a camp conversation or another discoverable record. An optional failed check should change cost, risk or interpretation rather than permanently block the final objective. Default to no unannounced deadlines; any missable or timed quest needs an explicit owner decision and legible warning.

### One concrete package example: Three Knocks

1. A side entrance contains fresh dust, an abandoned cup and audible tapping.
2. A short branch leads to a blocked chamber. The miner answers simple questions; a visible cracked support establishes the hazard.
3. The player can spend time bracing the route, use a suitable tool for a quicker safe action, or mark it for a camp recovery crew. A noisy shortcut may attract a local encounter after a warning.
4. Success records the miner's rescue and a vein clue. Securing/marking for a crew records a different state and pays off after the next return. Neither requires a particular hero class.
5. At camp, a short line acknowledges the chosen solution. On later days the module shows an empty cleared chamber or is omitted, and never pays its rescue reward twice.

This one package demonstrates discovery, a spatial scene, a choice, time/light cost, optional combat, a persistent NPC consequence and a camp payoff without needing an entirely separate game system.

## Long descent, manageable expeditions

Twelve floors must not mean walking through eleven freshly hostile floors before every new attempt. Extend the **existing secured-route concept** into a few earned chapter endpoints, rather than adding twelve independent fast-travel buttons. Possible milestones are the repaired early hoist, a secured waterworks station, the city approach and the temple precinct. These are route proposals, not free automatic travel or full underground shops.

Travel should have a clear cost/risk rule and arrive at the known safe entry, not bypass the current floor's challenge or secretly consume resources. A small relay cache or defensible rest area can help endurance; it does not need another nurse, merchant and recruitment fire. Decide whether a route unlock requires clearing its approach, delivering a mechanism or a camp supply payment before implementing it.

Avoid changing all floors uniformly just because the party grows. Each new layer adds a distinct problem: patrols, water routes, growth/control, ancient mechanisms, then combined temple choices. Keep earlier floor recognition and progress rewards. Some returns should be about finishing a quest or helping someone, not only gathering enough gold to repeat the same attempt.

The current XP system caps at level 4 and has allowances for floors 1–4. It cannot be assumed to support twelve floors by repeating its final lookup value. Plan later-act levels/talents, per-layer learning budgets, gear tiers, supply reach and replacement training together. A possible long-term target is a modest total such as 6–8 levels with more horizontal options, but that needs a separate balance proposal. This document does not increase levels, invent new attributes, reset earned progression or reuse the old four-floor simulation as proof for the expanded campaign.

## A practical first slice

Keep the built opening, camp and early cave/fortress available. Prove the richer structure with:

1. A short commander conversation after the escape, compatible with the selected hero and the established camp arrivals.
2. One visible early job: secure/recover the fallen party's remains and return with a meaningful clue.
3. One hidden area: Three Knocks, with two viable resolutions and a camp payoff.
4. One iron sample project at the apprentice, once he arrives.
5. A fortress service route that makes the queen a real side quest and offers a clear glimpse of the water layer below.

That tests the campaign's new purpose, package insertion, NPC dialogue, gathering, persistent rewards and an optional major fight. It does not require producing all twelve floors before finding out whether the idea works. #119's whole-campaign playtest is still useful evidence for these first four floors; the queen becomes a slice milestone rather than the final story ending.

## What remains to choose

- Twelve compact floors, fewer larger chapters, or a different length; the two-floor layer pattern is a recommendation.
- The relic's identity and the temple/portal's final function; what counts as resolving the deepest objective.
- The commander's actual role, cause of the ambush and rival motivations; all remain open despite suggested clues.
- Which activities are main-route steps, optional visible jobs and hidden discoveries; which choices can close a quest.
- Whether hidden quests have any undiscovered log indication; recommended default is none.
- Gathering quantities/project costs, route logistics and later-act progression.
- How much of each floor remains spatially stable across daily regeneration; the proposed minimum is stable identity/story outcomes with regenerated approaches.

All place names, quest titles and new NPC roles above are working concepts. The design should make each layer worth exploring while keeping one clear promise: the way down leads somewhere meaningful, and each return to camp changes what the party knows or can attempt.
